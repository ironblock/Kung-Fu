I wrote `/home/user/work/73f289/app/src/chase-camera.ts`, but I couldn't type-check it or run it, and part of it is a guess about the library's API. `createChaseCamera` makes a banked free camera, sets its position to `eye`, points it at `target`, and starts it upright with world +Y as up (+Z if the camera looks straight up or down). Each `bank(up)` call removes the part of `up` that lies along the line of sight, normalises what's left and sets that as the camera's `upVector`. If what's left is shorter than 1e-6, which is the case when `up` points exactly along the line of sight either way, the call does nothing. So position and target stay put, the camera keeps looking at the target, and it keeps its previous roll.

What I wasn't sure of:
- **Which factory to use.** The ledger mentions `createBankedFreeCamera` in its header comment, and says the banked camera has an `upVector` that marks the camera dirty when written. Plain free cameras are only described as having a tracked position and target. So I assumed a plain `FreeCamera` can't roll and used the banked factory.
- **Unverified API details.** Nothing in the ledger confirms that `createBankedFreeCamera` is exported from `@babylonjs/lite`, that it can be called with no arguments, or that what it returns can be typed as a `FreeCamera`. If any of those is wrong, the factory call on line 57 needs adjusting.
- **Writing coordinates one at a time.** I set x, y and z separately on `position`, `target` and `upVector` instead of replacing the objects. The only basis for this is an unchecked `is` row in the ledger saying such writes mark the camera dirty. Assigning a whole new object might lose that tracking, but the ledger doesn't say either way.
- **Which way is up.** "Upright" assumes Y is up. The ledger mentions a `#coordinate-system` module, so if your scene is set up differently (for example Z-up), the starting up direction should change to match.
