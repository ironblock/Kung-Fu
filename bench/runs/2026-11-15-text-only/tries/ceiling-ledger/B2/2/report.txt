I wrote /home/user/work/fba14c/app/src/fast-orbit.ts. It attaches the library's own `attachControl(camera, canvas, scene)` and adds two per-frame hooks. One goes at the front of `scene._beforeRender`, so it runs before the library's inertia step, and adds a second copy of any new drag input in `inertialAlphaOffset` and `inertialBetaOffset`. The other goes at the end and records the offsets the library leaves behind. Because the glide after release is just those offsets dying away, doubling every bit of input doubles both the drag and the glide. The radius offset (wheel zoom) is never touched. The returned function removes both hooks and then calls the library's own detach function, which removes its canvas listeners. Nothing was compiled or run, because the library isn't on this machine.

Things I'm not sure of:
- **How drag works inside the library.** The ledger rows that were checked confirm the frame order: hooks added by camera controls go at the end of the list, and the list runs from front to back. But the claim that drag input builds up in the public `inertialAlphaOffset` and `inertialBetaOffset` is an unchecked reader's note. If the library instead turns the camera directly in its pointer-move handler and only uses the offsets for the glide, my code would double only the glide. Nothing in the ledger mentions a sensitivity option on `attachControl`, so I didn't rely on one.
- **Use of internals.** I read and change the private `scene._beforeRender` list directly, and I assumed `attachControl` and the two offset fields are exported and named as the ledger says.
- **Clamping when looking up or down.** The doubled up-down offset goes through the library's own step, so its clamp (an unchecked note puts it at 0.01 from straight up or down) and any `setCameraLimits` limits still apply.
- **Detaching mid-frame.** If the returned function is called from inside a per-frame callback, removing our hooks makes the scene skip one neighbouring callback for that frame. The ledger notes the library's own detach does the same.
