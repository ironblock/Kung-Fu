I wrote `/home/user/work/b7c299/app/src/globe-tilt.ts`, but I'm not sure it holds the tilt at full zoom-out or when the app zooms out later. Nothing here has been compiled or run. The library isn't on this machine and the app has no other code to check against.

**What it does:** the function first loosens the camera's pitch limits. If `limits.pitchMax` or `limits.pitchMin` would stop the requested pitch, it moves them. If `limits.pitchDisabledRadiusScale` is set, it clears it. Then it sets `camera.pitch`, and leaves the centre, `yaw` and `radius` alone. It reads the pose back afterwards and throws if the pitch, zoom or heading isn't what it should be. It also throws a `RangeError` for a pitch outside 0 to just under π/2.

**What I wasn't sure of:**
- **Pitch limits.** The ledger has no checked rows on this. One unchecked note says that setting `pitch` keeps the centre, and that each orientation change clamps the angles and radius to `camera.limits`. That would explain a tilt not holding when zoomed out. The limit field names come from the full Babylon.js geospatial camera, not from Babylon Lite 1.39.0. That includes `pitchDisabledRadiusScale`, which in Babylon.js reduces pitch towards 0 as you zoom out. Clearing it to `undefined` assumes Lite treats it as optional. If Lite names these fields differently, they get skipped. The pitch can then still be clamped, and in that case the function throws an error rather than failing silently.
- **Pitch direction.** I assumed the library's pitch means the same as yours: 0 is straight down and larger values tilt towards the horizon.
- **Side effect.** Loosening the limits stays in place on that camera after the call. That's what keeps the tilt at other zoom levels, but it changes the camera's limits beyond this one tilt.
