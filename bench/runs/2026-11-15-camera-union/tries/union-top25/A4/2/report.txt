I wrote `notes/over-the-top.json` in the app with `eyeX` ≈ -4.794, `eyeY` ≈ 8.776, `upright` true, and `sameAsOtherSide` true. Nothing else in the app was changed.

The notes don't give the camera's position formula and the library isn't here to check, so these values assume Babylon's standard convention (x = r·cos(α)·sin(β), y = r·cos(β), z = r·sin(α)·sin(β), Y up). Under that convention, β = -0.5 is not clamped because no frame runs and the arc-rotate camera has no beta limits by default (note 8). It is the same point as α = π, β = 0.5, so the camera ends up on the other side of the top, with the picture still upright. The one thing I'd confirm against the real library is the position formula. The only difference from a fresh camera at α = π is a z value of about 1e-15, which is effectively identical.
