I read `camera-api.d.ts` and wrote `notes/inset-slide.json` with `{ "x": 800, "y": 300, "width": 300, "height": 150, "aspect": 2.00 }`. I worked these out by hand from the doc comments: each fraction times the canvas size, with `y` flipped to count from the top (500 − 0.4 × 500 = 300). The library isn't on this machine, so none of it was run.

**What I'm not sure of:**
- **Clamping:** The declarations don't say whether `resolveCameraViewport` clamps the rectangle to the canvas. Without clamping, the inset runs 100 px past the right edge (800 + 300 = 1100). WebGPU normally rejects viewports and scissor rectangles outside the render target, so the library may clamp somewhere. If `resolveCameraViewport` itself clamps, `width` would be 200, not 300.
- **Aspect:** The doc comment says the ratio comes from the normalized viewport, which gives 300 / 150 = 2.00, the same way Babylon.js does it. So the aspect most likely stays 2.00 even if the rectangle is clamped, but this is unconfirmed too.

If clamping happens anywhere, the frame and the picture probably won't line up: a 2:1 image drawn into the 200 × 150 px area left on the canvas would look squashed. So I'd plan to animate the inset differently, or check a single frame of the slide in the browser before deciding.
