I wrote `/home/dev/a4da93/app/src/center-on.ts`. It hasn't been compiled or run, because the library isn't on this machine. To find latitude 0, longitude 0, it creates a throwaway camera with the same planet radius and reads its centre. It then asks the library's own `computeLocalBasis` which way north and east point there, so positive longitude follows the camera's east and nothing about the axes is hard-coded. It builds the ground point from those directions, scales it to the planet radius, and calls `setGeospatialOrientation` with the camera's current yaw, pitch and radius plus the new centre.

Things I wasn't sure of:
- **Exports:** I assumed `computeLocalBasis` is exported from the `@babylonjs/lite` root. It's in the declarations without an underscore, so it looks public.
- **`Vec3`:** I assumed it's a plain `{ x, y, z }` object. If either assumption is wrong, it will show up as a compile error.
- **Heading:** I assumed a yaw of π/2 points along the library's `east` vector. Your heading-of-90° test should confirm that.
- **Moves in progress:** I don't know whether this cancels a camera move (`flyGeospatialCameraToAsync`) that's already running.
