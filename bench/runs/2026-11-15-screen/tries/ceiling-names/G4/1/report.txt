I wrote `src/spin-around.ts`. It runs four back-to-back quarter-turn flights with the library's `flyGeospatialCameraToAsync`, and each one follows its own slice of a single ease-in-out curve, so the spin is one smooth eastward turn instead of four starts and stops. At the end it sets `yaw` back to exactly its starting value. I split the turn up because asking for a target one full turn away gives the same heading you started at, and the fly function could then stay put or take the short way round.

I haven't type-checked or run any of this, since the library isn't here. The things I wasn't sure of:

- **Exports and behaviour:** I couldn't find a declaration for `SceneContext`, so the fly function is the only render-loop driver I could confirm. I'm assuming it and `setGeospatialOrientation` are exported from the `@babylonjs/lite` root. I'm also assuming the fly function applies `ease` to its progress, lands exactly on its target and leaves fields you don't pass unchanged. I set `centerHopScale: 0` on the guess that it means no zoom-out hop.
- **Interruptions:** if a quarter-turn doesn't land on its target, the spin stops there and the promise still resolves, without going back to the start heading. That happens if the user grabs the controls, another flight starts, or `limits.yawMin`/`yawMax` are narrower than a full turn. I chose resolve over reject so code waiting on the intro carries on, but that's your call.
- **Small details:** there may be a one-frame hitch where the quarter-turns join, and the total time can run a few frames over `durationMs`. A duration of zero or less rejects with a `RangeError`.
