I wrote `notes/inset-slide.json` with the viewport result `{ x: 800, y: 300, width: 200, height: 150 }`. I set `aspect` to `null` because the notes don't settle what `getEffectiveAspectRatio` returns here, and I didn't want to put a guess in the file.

**Viewport.** These numbers come straight from note 15. x = 0.8 × 1000 = 800. The width would be 300, but the right edge is clamped to the canvas, so it becomes 1000 − 800 = 200. Height = 0.3 × 500 = 150. The notes say `viewport.y` is counted from the bottom while the returned y is counted from the top, so y = 500 − 50 − 150 = 300.

**Aspect.** The notes only say to use this value as the aspect ratio (note 16). They don't say whether it uses the clamped rectangle or the unclamped one, and that choice decides your question:
- **Unclamped (300 × 150):** 2.00. The picture is projected for a 2:1 shape while your frame is 200 × 150 (1.33). The two won't line up, and the image would likely look squashed or cropped during the slide.
- **Clamped (200 × 150):** 1.33. The frame and the picture match in size, but the content gets reframed as the inset slides, rather than moving across as one fixed image.

I couldn't run the library to check. The quickest way to find out is to log `getEffectiveAspectRatio(camera, 1000, 500)` once in the browser at this point in the slide and put that number in the file.
