I wrote `src/center-on.ts`. It converts the latitude and longitude to a point on the sphere of radius `camera.limits.planetRadius`, using the camera's own frame from notes 15 and 16. In that frame, latitude 0, longitude 0 is +X, north is +Z and east is −Y. It then gives `camera.center` a new object, because note 10 says only replacing the whole object moves the camera at once. Heading, tilt and zoom are not touched.

Nothing has been compiled or run, since the library isn't here. A few things I'm not sure of:

- **The `Vec3` type:** its declaration (`math/types`) isn't in the file you gave me. I assumed it's a plain `{ x, y, z }` object. If it's a class or has more fields, TypeScript will report an error at the line that builds the centre.
- **Limits changed by hand:** note 24 says assigning `center` applies any limit change made since the last yaw/pitch/radius write. If your code does that, zoom or tilt could shift when you centre.
- **A running flight:** I didn't stop a `flyGeospatialCameraToAsync` flight that's still going, so it would likely overwrite the new centre on the next frame. The only way I can see to stop one is the internal `_cancelFly` field, so I left it alone.
- **East away from the start point:** that a 90° heading faces positive longitude everywhere, not just at 0,0, assumes the camera works out east the same way at every point. The notes only state it for the starting position.
