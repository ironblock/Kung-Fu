I wrote the answer to `/home/dev/f6de18/app/notes/over-the-top.json`: the eye is at about (-4.794, 8.776, 0), the picture is upright, and the view matches the fresh camera at `(PI, 0.5)`.

The notes don't cover the alpha/beta-to-position formula or the up vector, so I used Babylon's standard arc-rotate convention. Beta isn't clamped on a direct write, so -0.5 stays as written. With that convention, (alpha, beta) and (alpha + PI, -beta) land on the same point, which is why the two views match. Two things to check against the library: the position formula, and whether the up vector stays world +Y. Also, `sameAsOtherSide` is true to any displayable precision, but not bit-for-bit: `Math.sin(Math.PI)` is about 1.2e-16, so the other side's z is about 6e-16 rather than 0.
