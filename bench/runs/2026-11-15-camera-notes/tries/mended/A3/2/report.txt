I wrote `app/src/screen-shift.ts`. `mark()` copies the camera's view and view-projection matrices, and `shift()` projects the point through both the marked and current matrices onto the canvas as it is now, returning the CSS-pixel difference. I could not compile or run it because the library isn't on this machine, so the following are unverified:

- I assumed `getViewMatrix`, `getViewProjectionMatrix`, `getEffectiveAspectRatio`, `resolveCameraViewport`, and `projectWorldToScreen` are exported from the `"@babylonjs/lite"` root. The declarations you gave are internal file paths, so check that.
- I did not handle floating origin (`_useFloatingOrigin` and the `worldOrigin` option). I don't know what origin the library uses, so cameras with floating origin may give wrong shifts.
- I did not handle points behind the camera, since you said points are in front.
