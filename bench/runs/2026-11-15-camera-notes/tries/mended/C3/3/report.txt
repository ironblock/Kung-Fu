I could not answer these three questions from the material here, so `notes/arrow-keys.json` has `null` in all three fields rather than guessed numbers. The notes say held arrows add `1/angularSensitivity` per frame "normalised", but not whether the two keys are normalised together or per axis. That decides the answer: a combined normalisation would give about 0.71 for the rotate and alt fractions and make the Ctrl pan distance the same, while per-axis handling would give 1.00 and a longer diagonal pan. The Up key also lowers beta, which has clamps, so the result can depend on the starting beta.

To get real values, run the 60-frame test in an environment that has `@babylonjs/lite` 1.39.0, with `keyboard: true` and the scene running. Record the change in `alpha` for Right alone and Right+Up, with and without Alt, and the target's displacement for Ctrl+Right alone and Ctrl+Right+Up. Those ratios go into the file.
