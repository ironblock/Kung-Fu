I used the standard Babylon arc-rotate formula, `x = r·cos(α)·sin(β)`, `y = r·cos(β)`, `z = r·sin(α)·sin(β)`, which gives the position above. Two things to confirm if you need certainty:

- **Upright** is `true` on the assumption that the camera builds its view with world +Y as the up vector, as Babylon does. Nothing in the declarations says otherwise.
- **`sameAsOtherSide`** is `true` in the geometric sense. The fresh camera at α = π has `z` ≈ 6e-16 from floating-point rounding, while the flipped camera has `z` = 0 exactly. So the two views are identical on screen but not bit-for-bit equal.
