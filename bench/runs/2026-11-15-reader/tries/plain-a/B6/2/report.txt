It wraps the checkout's `createBankedFreeCamera` and sets its `upVector` to the car's up on each `bank(up)` call. The library's look-at already projects that vector perpendicular to the line of sight, so the picture-up matches it as closely as possible.

Two things I was unsure about:
- On a degenerate call, the library would reset to an identity rotation, so `bank()` skips the write and the previous roll is kept. "Degenerate" means the sine of the angle between up and the line of sight is at most 1e-6. That threshold is my choice. A tighter one would let float noise in the car's up pick an arbitrary roll near the parallel case, and a looser one would freeze the roll for real up directions. Adjust it if your car's up is noisier or cleaner than I assumed.
- I checked behaviour with a temporary vitest file (6 cases: upright start, tilted roll, projection, degenerate keeps roll both ways, no NaN, parity with the library's own banking) and removed it afterwards, so `src/` contains only `chase-camera.ts`. `tsc` reports no errors in `src/`; its errors all come from the babylon-lite checkout's own shader and worker imports.
