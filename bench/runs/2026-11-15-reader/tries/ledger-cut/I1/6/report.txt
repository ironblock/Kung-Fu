Reasoning, from the source in babylon-lite/packages/babylon-lite:
- The transition driver (animation/frame-interpolation.ts) runs once per frame from scene._beforeRender. Its first step does not check for interference. Every later step throws if camera alpha, beta, radius or target differs from what the transition last wrote, and a throw rejects the promise.
- attachControl's applyInertia (camera/arc-rotate-controls.ts) is pushed onto _beforeRender at attach time, so it runs before any transition registered later. The first transition step zeroes all inertial offsets.
- (a) Coasting: applyInertia may move the camera once in the first frame, but the transition's first step has no check and zeroes the inertia. After that nothing changes the pose, so it resolves.
- (b) Drag: pointer moves add to the inertial offsets, and applyInertia then changes alpha/beta/target before the transition's check on the next frame. The pose differs, so it throws and rejects.
- (c) Second call: the second driver's first step writes a new pose after the first driver's write in the same frame. On the next frame the first driver sees a pose that differs from its last write and rejects. The second call's driver owns the camera from then on and resolves.
