It builds the camera with the library's `createBankedFreeCamera`, which the checkout exports, and on each `bank(up)` call it projects the car's up onto the view plane and writes that to `camera.upVector`. A call where that projection is near zero (up along the line of sight, either way, or eye exactly on target) leaves the camera and its roll unchanged.

Things I was not fully sure of:
- The degenerate threshold is 1e-6, not the library's own 1e-10. I chose the larger value so that float32-rounded inputs that are exactly parallel to the view direction are caught instead of producing an arbitrary roll. Inputs that are only slightly off-parallel (below 1e-6) keep the previous roll rather than rolling by a noisy amount.
- If the eye sits directly above or below the target at creation, the library's initial basis is the identity rotation, not upright. The stored roll starts at world +Y, so the first valid `bank` call still works, but the camera is not upright until then.
