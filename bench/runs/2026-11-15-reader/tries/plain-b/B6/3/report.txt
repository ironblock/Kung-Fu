I wrote `src/chase-camera.ts` in the app, exporting `createChaseCamera(eye, target)` and the `ChaseCamera` interface as specified. It is built on the checkout's `createBankedFreeCamera`, which exposes a mutable `upVector`. Each `bank(up)` normalizes `up`, projects it onto the plane perpendicular to the line of sight, and writes the result to `upVector`, so the picture-up is the closest achievable direction to the car's up. If the projection is below 1e-9 (up along the line of sight, either way), or the eye sits on the target, it returns without writing, which keeps the previous roll and the target in view. I also added `src/chase-camera.test.ts`, which covers the upright start, the roll matching the projected up, and keeping the roll on parallel calls; `vitest run` passes 3/3. `tsc` reports no errors in the app's files; its only errors are inside babylon-lite's own build-time imports (`?raw`, `?worker`, WebXR types), which were already there.

Things I was not fully sure of: the 1e-9 threshold is a float tolerance rather than an exact-zero test, so "exactly" parallel is taken to mean "within numerical noise". Calling `bank()` again after the eye and target have moved keeps the old `upVector` rather than re-deriving a roll, which I read as what "keep its previous roll" means. I did not run this in a browser or with real car data.
