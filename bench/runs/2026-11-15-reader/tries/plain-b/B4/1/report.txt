I wrote /tmp/claude-0/-home-user/00000000-0000-0000-0000-000000000000/scratchpad/reader-work/plain-b/B4/1/app/src/globe-tilt.ts. It exports `tiltGlobeCamera(camera, pitch)`, which sets `camera.limits.pitchDisabledRadiusScale = null` and then `camera.pitch = pitch`. No other files were changed.

Why this is needed: in the checked-out Babylon Lite (`packages/babylon-lite/src/camera/geospatial-limits.ts`), `getEffectivePitchMax` lowers the allowed pitch as the radius grows past 2x the planet radius, and at the default fully zoomed-out radius (4x) it allows only the minimum pitch (about 0.001). A plain `camera.pitch = 0.6` on a new camera therefore ends at about 0.001. Turning that feature off makes the full pitch range available at every radius, so the tilt holds when the app zooms. Zoom, yaw and centre are not changed by the call.

Verification: I ran a temporary vitest suite (since deleted). It covered a fresh camera at the default radius, nonzero yaw, zoom changes from 4R down to 10 m, and a centre off the equator. In each case the view direction matched the requested pitch, and radius, yaw and centre were unchanged. A baseline check confirmed that without the change the pitch was clamped. The app's `tsc` run showed no errors in `globe-tilt.ts`; the only errors are pre-existing WebXR type gaps inside the library's `xr/` files.

Things I am not sure of: (1) Turning off `pitchDisabledRadiusScale` removes the library's behaviour of tilting toward straight down when zoomed out. If the app relies on that elsewhere, this is a behaviour change, and the function affects only that camera's limits object. (2) If the centre is within about 0.1° of a pole, the library's pole clamp will move the centre slightly on any orientation change. I did not handle that case.
