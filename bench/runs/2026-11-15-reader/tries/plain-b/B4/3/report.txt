What it does: the library fades allowed pitch toward 0 as the camera zooms out (`limits.pitchDisabledRadiusScale`, default `{x: 2, y: 4}`). A new camera rests at `radiusMax` (4 planet radii), so a plain pitch assignment there is clamped flat, and a later zoom-out would permanently clamp the stored pitch. The function therefore sets `camera.limits.pitchDisabledRadiusScale = null` and then calls `setGeospatialOrientation(camera, { pitch })`, which leaves yaw, radius and centre unchanged.

Things I was not sure of:
- Turning off the pitch fade is a per-camera change in behaviour. Any other code on that camera that relies on zooming out to look straight down will no longer get that. I did not check the controls loop for that interaction.
- Pitch values are not range-checked. Anything above `limits.pitchMax` (π/2 − 0.01) is clamped by the library; your 0.1–1.2 range is fine.
