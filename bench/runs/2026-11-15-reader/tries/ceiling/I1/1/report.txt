I worked them out from the checked-out library source and then confirmed all three with a test that passes.

- **(a) Resolves.** On its first frame the transition sets all of the camera's leftover coasting speed to zero. The controls' per-frame update runs before the transition's step, and from then on it has nothing left to apply, so the camera only moves when the transition moves it. The camera reaches the goal and the promise resolves. The doc comment in `arc-rotate-interpolate.ts` lists "decaying inertia" as something that interrupts the transition. That is misleading here: coasting that was already going when the call is made does not cause a rejection.
- **(b) Rejects.** A mouse drag feeds into the camera's coasting speed. On the next frame the controls move the camera, the transition sees a pose it did not write, and its promise rejects with "ArcRotate camera interpolation was interrupted."
- **(c) The first call rejects.** The second call's first frame moves the camera, so on the next frame the first transition sees a pose it did not write and rejects. The second call carries on and resolves.

So the try/catch belongs around cases (b) and (c), the drag and the repeated `goHome()`.
