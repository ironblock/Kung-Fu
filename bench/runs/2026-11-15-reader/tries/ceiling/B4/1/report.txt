Before setting the pitch it switches off the library's rule that flattens the tilt as you zoom out (`camera.limits.pitchDisabledRadiusScale = null`). It then sets only the pitch, passing the current heading, zoom and centre through unchanged. I also added a test, /tmp/claude-0/-home-user/5a6488dd-41c7-5be7-b55c-ee990999c7a9/scratchpad/reader-work/ceiling/B4/1/app/src/globe-tilt.test.ts. It covers pitches from 0.1 to 1.2, starting from a new camera at full zoom-out, and then moves the camera to several distances. All 5 tests pass with `npm test`.

Why the fix is needed: by default this version narrows the allowed tilt as the camera moves away from the globe, until it reaches zero at full zoom-out (4× the planet radius). So setting the pitch on a new camera alone gives a camera looking straight down. Even after a successful tilt, zooming out would clamp it away, and since the clamped value is what the camera keeps, it would stay flat when zoomed back in. Nothing in the library checkout was changed.

Things you should know:
- **Side effect:** turning off the zoom-out flattening is a change to the camera's own settings, not just this one call. Any later tilt by the user or by fly-to animations will also keep its angle at full zoom-out.
- **Pitch bounds:** if your app has narrowed the camera's allowed pitch range below or above the value you pass, the function widens it to fit that value. The defaults (about 0.001 to 1.56) already cover 0.1 to 1.2, so normally this does nothing.
- **Rounding at Earth scale:** with an Earth-sized globe, the camera matrix the renderer uses is rounded more coarsely than the camera's own numbers. It still points at the centre at the right tilt, within about 0.001 rad. That is ordinary rounding, so the test only checks the matrix to that tolerance.
- **Type check:** running `tsc` on the app reports errors, but they are all missing WebXR type names inside the library's own `xr/` files, not in the new code.
