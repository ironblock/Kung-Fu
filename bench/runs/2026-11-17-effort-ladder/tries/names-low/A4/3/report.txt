I computed the arc-rotate camera's world position from the spherical-coordinate formula (standard Babylon convention) with alpha=0, beta=−0.5, radius=10, target at origin, then checked whether the up vector still projects upright and whether the resulting view matches a camera at (π, 0.5). No surprises: negative beta simply places the camera below the XZ plane on the same α side, the picture stays upright, and it is not the same view as the (π, 0.5) camera (which is above the plane on the opposite side). I'm confident in the position math; the only assumption is that Babylon Lite uses the same spherical formula and fixed (0,1,0) up vector as full Babylon.js, which is strongly implied by the type declarations showing no custom up-vector or alternative projection.
